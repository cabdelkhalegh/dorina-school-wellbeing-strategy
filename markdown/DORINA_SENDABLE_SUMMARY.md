## 1. Why now — what the research says

Three facts came out of the research, and together they describe a door that is open right now:

**First, the need is documented and enormous.** UNICEF's 2025 assessment found that after the 2024 escalation, **72% of caregivers in Lebanon reported their children anxious or nervous, and 62% reported them sad or depressed** ([UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/press-releases/decline-childrens-mental-health-nutrition-and-education-after-war-lebanon-unicef)). This is not a niche concern — it is the majority of families. Schools feel it every day: anxious children, exhausted parents, teachers carrying emotional weight they were never trained for. Schools know they need help. Most have no practical, parent-facing answer.

**Second, the buyers can pay, and wellbeing is what they compete on.** Lebanon has one of the world's highest private-school enrollment rates — over 1,600 private schools — and Beirut's premium tier is now dollarized, with top schools charging $14,000–22,000 per year. These schools compete for enrollment on holistic offerings. A year-round wellbeing program is exactly the kind of differentiator they budget for.

**Third, the current is moving in your direction.** The Ministry of Public Health's National Mental Health Strategy 2024–2030 explicitly aims to connect schools with community wellbeing support, and MEHE is already running psychosocial support programming with UNESCO in 50 public schools. When the state itself invests in school wellbeing, private schools feel both permission and pressure to act. You would not be pushing against the current — you'd be riding it.

And here is the gap: NGOs serve vulnerable public-school populations on grant cycles. Clinical services are individual and, for many families, carry stigma. School counselors are stretched thin and student-facing. **Almost nobody offers what Beirut's schools actually need to buy: a bilingual, parent-focused, coaching-based, non-clinical wellbeing partner who can stand in front of forty Arabic- and English-speaking parents on a Tuesday evening and give them practical tools.**

That is you. That lane is empty.

## 2. The positioning — one sentence, three tracks

Everything in the plan hangs on one sentence, and I'd like it to become yours:

> **"Dorina helps schools and families strengthen children's emotional wellbeing through practical parent–child support, coaching-based tools, and bilingual workshops adapted to each school community."**
>
> **«دورينا تساعد المدارس والعائلات على تعزيز الصحة النفسية والتوازن العاطفي للأطفال من خلال أدوات عملية للتواصل بين الأهل والأبناء، وورش عمل ثنائية اللغة مصمّمة لكل مجتمع مدرسي.»**

The public umbrella is **Parent & Child Emotional Wellbeing Workshops for Schools in Beirut** (with a premium variant, *School–Family Emotional Wellbeing Programs*, for the top-tier schools). Under it sit three tracks:

1. **Parent Workshops** — communication, emotional regulation at home, discipline without conflict, supporting anxious children, confidence, school stress. Seven complete workshops are already designed, each with learning outcomes, a minute-by-minute agenda, and bilingual titles.
2. **Child Support Workshops** — self-confidence, emotional expression, stress and worry skills, focus, friendship skills, resilience. Six series are designed, all in small groups, always with school staff present and written parental consent — that rule is non-negotiable and it is one of your strongest selling points.
3. **School Partnership — the flagship.** Annual agreements: monthly parent sessions, child workshop series, teacher sessions, awareness campaigns, and a wellbeing calendar co-designed with each school from October to June.

One positioning decision matters more than any other, and I want to be transparent about it. **We never lead with hypnotherapy in school-facing material.** Schools can be sensitive to therapy language, and one wrong first impression can close a door that took weeks to open. Your credentials — **Master in Clinical Hypnotherapy, Master in NLP / Coaching, Master in Time Line Therapy** — appear in every serious conversation, exactly worded, but they appear as *credibility proof*, after the value is clear: "My training includes…" This isn't hiding anything; it's sequencing it. And alongside it sits a firm ethical line that protects you: no clinical claims, no diagnosis, no replacing school counselors — you complement them, and you refer families to licensed professionals whenever a child needs more than a workshop can give. Schools trust the expert who says this out loud. It will win you deals.

## 3. How the business works — a ladder, not a stage

The plan is deliberately not built on one-off talks. It is built on a ladder:

**Free or low-cost intro talk → paid workshop → term package → annual partnership.**

The intro talk (a 60–90 minute evening for parents, *"Raising Confident Kids in Stressful Times"*) opens the door — free for the first three to five premium schools only, a token fee elsewhere. Every talk ends with feedback forms completed in the room and a follow-up meeting booked before you leave the building. The paid workshop proves value. The term package builds rhythm. And the **annual partnership — roughly $2,500 to $18,000 per year depending on tier and school segment — is the real business**: recurring, calendar-embedded, and hard to displace once you're part of a school's year.

Two honest caveats, because I'd rather you hear them from me: **all prices are working ranges** that we validate together in the first ten school conversations, and Lebanon's payment mechanics (fresh dollars, transfers, cash) get confirmed school by school. The ranges are grounded in what the market can carry, but the market gets the final word.

The math that matters: one mid-tier partnership equals ten to twenty individual workshops sold — with one negotiation, one contract, and one relationship. Five partnerships can outperform fifty scattered talks. That's why every rung of the ladder is designed to sell the next one.

The window is **27 July to 25 October 2026**, timed precisely to the Lebanese school calendar:

**Phase 1 — August (Days 1–30): build.** Schools run on summer rhythm, so we build the arsenal: your bio, one-pager, pitch deck, feedback forms, safeguarding statement, consent forms, photos, LinkedIn rewrite, and the finalized bilingual workshop content. In parallel we build a list of 30 named target schools across the three segments and map every warm connection we have to it. **Phase 2 — September (Days 31–60): reach out.** Back-to-school planning season — the single best selling window of the year. We contact 20 schools, always warm-intro first (Beirut runs on relationships, and cold email alone produces nothing), hold 8 discovery meetings, and book 3–5 intro talks for late September and October. Every script — WhatsApp, email, LinkedIn, in Arabic and English — is already written and waiting for your approval.

**Phase 3 — October (Days 61–90): deliver and convert.** You deliver 3 pilot workshops. We collect feedback data, secure 2 testimonials, present partnership proposals built on each school's *own parents' feedback* — and close the first signed partnership by day 90. Alongside: one media or podcast appearance, timed to the back-to-school-anxiety story every outlet wants in September.

By day 90, success looks like: **30 schools listed · 20 contacted · 8 discovery meetings · 3 pilots delivered · 1 signed annual partnership · parent satisfaction ≥ 8.5/10 · 2 testimonials · a rebuilt LinkedIn presence · 1 media appearance.** Ambitious, and honestly achievable — the weekly plan behind it accounts for August slowness, school budget cycles, and even an online-delivery fallback for every session if the situation in the country disrupts in-person work.
