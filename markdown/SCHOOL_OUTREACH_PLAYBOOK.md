## 2. Decision-Maker Map — Who Signs, Who Champions, Who Blocks

| Role | What they care about | Power | Contact order |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Principal / Head of School** | School reputation, parent satisfaction, differentiation, safeguarding, cost | Final signature on partnerships | First in **community schools** (they decide alone, fast) |
| **Head of Elementary / Cycle Director** | Age-appropriate content, timetable fit, teacher buy-in | Strong recommender; often the real evaluator in premium schools | First or second in **premium schools** |
| **School Counselor / Psychologist** | Student welfare, professional boundaries, not being undermined | Veto power. Can kill a deal silently | Engage **early and respectfully** — see below |
| **Parent Committee Head** | Value for parents, practical help, event success | Door-opener; can fund small events from committee budget | Parallel warm route |
| **NGO Program Manager** | Funder deliverables, safeguarding compliance, measurable outcomes | Signs project-based contracts | First contact in NGO segment |

### The counselor: ally, never gatekeeper — how to approach

The counselor is the person most likely to feel threatened by an external "wellbeing expert." Handle this deliberately:

1. **Never position around them; position beside them.** Standard line: *"My work is coaching-based and preventive — parent skills and child confidence. Anything clinical belongs with your counselor, and part of my role is to strengthen referrals to them."*
2. **Ask for their input before the pilot** — offer a 20-minute call to align content with what they see in students.
3. **Name them in the room.** In every parent workshop, publicly direct parents to the school counselor as the first port of call.
4. **Share materials in advance.** No surprises.

If the principal is met first, always say: *"Before we run anything, I'd like your counselor's eyes on the content."* This sentence wins deals.

### Who to contact first, per segment

- **Premium schools:** warm intro to Principal or Head of Elementary → counselor alignment call → parent-committee awareness.
- **Community schools:** Principal directly (often owner-director) — decisions in one or two meetings.
- **NGOs:** Program Manager (education/child protection portfolio), referencing funder-relevant outcomes.
- **Parent committees:** Committee head via WhatsApp warm intro; goal is a free/low-cost talk that creates internal pull.

## 3. Channel Strategy for Lebanon

Beirut is relationship-driven. Cold email alone will produce near-zero results. Channel priority:

1. **Warm introductions (always first).** Before any cold touch, Dorina and AK map mutual contacts: parents at target schools, alumni networks, former colleagues, family friends, professionals who serve schools (pediatricians, speech therapists, activity providers). One warm intro is worth 20 cold emails.
2. **WhatsApp (dominant channel).** Most Lebanese school administrators live on WhatsApp. Use WhatsApp Business with a professional profile (name, logo, one-line description, catalog link). Rules: short messages, no voice notes on first touch, send 9:00–13:00 or 16:00–19:00, never Sunday, never after 20:30.
3. **Email.** For premium schools and NGOs — the "formal record" channel. Attach the one-pager PDF (< 2 MB). Follow every meaningful WhatsApp exchange with a confirming email.
4. **LinkedIn.** For premium-school leadership and NGO managers; also the credibility backdrop (they *will* check Dorina's profile before replying — profile rewrite precedes outreach; see 90-day plan Weeks 3–4).
5. **School events & fairs.** Back-to-school open days (late Sept–Oct), education fairs, NGO conferences: attend, don't pitch hard; collect three names per event.

**Etiquette rules (non-negotiable):**
- Greet properly and use titles (Madame la Directrice / حضرة المدير / Dr. / Mrs.). Formality first; warmth follows.
- Never pressure for a same-week decision; propose "a short coffee or call whenever suits you."
- Mirror language: reply in the language they use; open bilingual when unsure.
- Ramadan/holiday calendars and any security disruptions: pause promotional touches, send only warm greetings.
- Never mention another school's name as a client without written permission.

## 5. Discovery Meeting — 45 Minutes That Win the Pilot

### 5.1 Minute-by-minute agenda

| Time | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:05 | Warm opening | Thank them; genuine compliment about the school (specific — a program, reputation, campus). Confirm time available. |
| 0:05–0:10 | Frame | "I'd love to spend most of this hearing about your school and families; then I'll share briefly how I work; and if it makes sense, we agree on a simple next step." |
| 0:10–0:25 | Discovery | Work through questions (below). Listen 80%, note verbatim phrases — they become proposal language. |
| 0:25–0:33 | Tailored presentation | Present ONLY the track matching what they said. Show one-pager + 2–3 workshop titles. Mention counselor-collaboration principle explicitly. |
| 0:33–0:38 | Credentials & safeguards | Approach (bilingual, practical, non-clinical), training background (exact wording), consent forms, staff-present rule, referral policy. |
| 0:38–0:43 | Pilot proposal + close | Propose specific pilot with **two candidate dates**. Handle immediate questions. |
| 0:43–0:45 | Wrap | Recap agreements, confirm who sends what by when, thank them, ask for counselor's contact for the alignment call. |

### 5.2 Twelve discovery questions

1. What are the school's top two or three priorities for this academic year?
2. What are you hearing from parents lately — what worries them most about their children?
3. How would you describe parent engagement here — attendance at events, responsiveness, involvement?
4. What's the biggest frustration on the parent-engagement side?
5. What wellbeing or psychosocial support does the school currently offer students and families?
6. How does your counselor currently work with parents, and where do they wish they had more support?
7. Have you run external workshops or speakers for parents before? What worked, what didn't?
8. Which age groups or cycles feel the most pressing need this year?
9. How do Arabic/English/French preferences run across your parent community?
10. How does the school typically budget for parent programming or external providers — and when is that budget set?
11. If we ran a pilot session and it went well, what would the decision process look like for something ongoing — who's involved?
12. What would need to be true, by June, for you to call a wellbeing partnership a clear success?

## 6. Pilot-to-Partnership Conversion Flow

The pilot is not the product; the partnership is. Everything in the pilot is engineered to produce three artifacts: **feedback scores, testimonials, and a debrief meeting.**

### 6.1 Pilot delivery checklist

**Before (1 week out):** confirmation email with outline sent; counselor alignment call done; consent/communication sent to parents by school; room/projector/language mix confirmed; feedback forms printed (AR/EN); roll-up or slide with Dorina's name and the umbrella title ready.
**Day of:** arrive 45 min early; greet principal and counselor personally; welcome slide bilingual; deliver 90 min with 2 interactive exercises minimum; close with "one tool you'll use tonight" round; distribute feedback form in-room (target 80% completion — never "email it later"); collect verbal permission from 2–3 enthusiastic parents to quote them; photo of the room (audience from behind, no children's faces, school's permission).
**Within 48h:** thank-you WhatsApp to principal + counselor; tally NPS and comments; send feedback summary + debrief meeting request.

### 6.2 Feedback form capture (the conversion engine)

Form fields (bilingual, one page): overall value 1–10 (NPS source, target ≥ 8.5); "one thing you'll do differently at home"; "what topic should come next?"; "may we quote your feedback?" (name optional); "would you attend a monthly session?" yes/no. The "topic next" and "monthly?" answers become the partnership proposal's evidence base.

### 6.3 Debrief meeting script (30 min, within 7 days of pilot)

> "Thank you again for hosting the session. Let me share what your parents said: the average value score was [X]/10, [N]% said they'd attend monthly sessions, and the most requested next topics were [A] and [B]. A few comments, in their words: [2 quotes]. From my side, what I noticed in the room was [genuine observation]. My question to you: what did *your* team think?"
> *(Listen. Then:)*
> "Given the response, here's what a year could look like: [present Essentials / Growth tier matching their budget signals — monthly parent sessions, child workshop series, teacher–parent collaboration session, an annual calendar we design together]. ### 6.5 Negotiation guardrails

1. **Never free beyond the pilot.** One complimentary/symbolic-fee session per school, ever. Second session onward is paid — even a small amount. Line: *"The introductory session was my investment in the relationship; ongoing work is priced so I can deliver it at full quality."*
2. **Scholarship seats, not discounts.** If cost objections come on parent-paid models: *"Rather than lowering the standard, I include [N] sponsored seats per session for families the school nominates."* Protects price integrity and serves the mission.
3. **Trade money for scope, not price for nothing.** Budget short? Remove a component (fewer child series, per-term instead of monthly) — do not cut the rate.
4. **Anchor with Growth, fall back to Essentials.** Never open with Essentials.
5. **Termly payment is fine; per-session drip pricing is not** — partnerships are annual commitments with termly invoicing.
6. **All figures are working ranges to validate in the first 10 conversations** — log every price reaction in the tracker.
