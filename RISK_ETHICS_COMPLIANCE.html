Risk, Ethics & Compliance Framework

Parent & Child Emotional Wellbeing Workshops for Schools in Beirut

Who this document is for: Dorina [Last Name] and the team supporting her school-facing practice. It is a working professional standard, not legal advice. Items marked [Legal review] must be checked with a Lebanese lawyer/accountant before use in contracts.

How to read it: Nothing in this document is designed to limit Dorina. Every rule here is a trust-builder — the exact things principals, counselors, and parent committees look for before they let anyone work with their children. Schools that see this framework will sign faster, not slower.

1. Purpose & Scope — Why Ethics Is a Commercial Asset

Schools in Lebanon are gatekeepers for their children and their reputation. After years of crisis, parents and administrators are simultaneously more aware of children's emotional needs (UNICEF's 2025 rapid assessment found 72% of caregivers reporting anxious or nervous children, and 62% reporting sadness or depression after the 2024 escalation) and more protective about who is allowed near those needs. The providers who win multi-year school partnerships are the ones who arrive with safeguarding, consent, and scope-of-practice already answered — before the school has to ask.

This framework therefore serves three commercial purposes:

  1. It shortens the sales cycle. A one-page safeguarding summary attached to a proposal removes the single biggest silent objection a principal has.
  2. It protects the flagship offer. Track 3 (annual School Partnership) is a trust product. One boundary incident, one overclaimed result, or one misframed headline ("hypnotist in schools") could end the practice. This document prevents all three.
  3. It positions Dorina as complementary, never competitive. Lebanon's Ministry of Public Health National Mental Health Strategy 2024–2030 explicitly aims to link schools with community mental health services. Dorina's practice fits inside that national direction as a wellbeing and skills layer that feeds referrals to licensed professionals — the safest and most fundable position in the market.

Scope of this document: all school-facing activity in Lebanon (workshops, partnerships, talks, campaigns), all marketing that mentions schools or children, and all data collected through that work. Private 1:1 adult family coaching outside schools follows the same spirit with lighter mechanics.

2. Professional Boundaries & Scope of Practice

Dorina's school offer is coaching-based, educational, and preventive. It builds skills; it does not treat conditions. This line must be held in every conversation, contract, slide, and social post.

2.1 Scope table

Dorina DOES (in schools) Dorina DOES NOT do (in schools)
Deliver parent workshops: communication, emotional regulation at home, discipline without conflict, supporting anxious children, confidence, school stress Diagnose any mental health, behavioral, learning, or medical condition
Deliver child skills workshops (group, staff present): self-confidence, emotional expression, everyday stress and worry skills, focus, social skills, resilience Treat anxiety, depression, trauma, ADHD, or any condition; provide psychotherapy, counseling-as-treatment, or medical care
Run teacher–parent collaboration sessions and school wellbeing campaigns Replace or substitute for school counselors, school psychologists, or special-education staff
Provide psychoeducation: how emotions work, how parents can support children, when and how to seek licensed help Provide crisis intervention, risk assessment, or emergency mental health response
Refer families warmly to licensed professionals, the school counselor, and Embrace Lifeline 1564 when signs suggest more support is needed Accept individual child "cases," conduct 1:1 child sessions, or work with a child outside a supervised group setting
Co-design annual wellbeing calendars with school leadership Promise measurable clinical outcomes ("reduces anxiety by X%") or before/after mental-health change

2.2 The hypnotherapy rule (non-negotiable)

  • Hypnotherapy techniques are never used in school settings. No trance work, no guided hypnotic inductions, no "hypno-anything" with children or in school-branded programs. Relaxation and breathing exercises used in workshops are standard, transparent wellbeing tools — always described as such.
  • Dorina's "Master in Clinical Hypnotherapy" is stated only as background training in her full credentials list ("Dorina's training includes: Master in Clinical Hypnotherapy, Master in NLP / Coaching, Master in Time Line Therapy") — never as a service offered to schools, never in a headline, never in the first line of any school-facing copy.
  • If a school asks about it directly, the answer is simple and confident: "That's part of my professional training background. In schools I work only with coaching-based, educational tools — everything we do is transparent, group-based, and skills-focused."

2.3 Why this protects the business

Holding this line does not shrink the offer — it is the offer. Schools already have (or want) counselors; they do not have a bilingual, parent-focused, practical wellbeing partner. The clear boundary is what makes counselors allies instead of blockers, and allies inside the school are how annual partnerships get renewed.

3. Sensitive Terminology Guide

Language is the first safeguarding tool. In Lebanon, mental-health stigma is real but softening; the accepted public framing is الصحة النفسية (mental/psychological wellbeing) used in a normalizing, non-clinical register — the register used by MoPH and Embrace. The rules: never clinical, never fear-based, never implying the child is "broken."

3.1 AVOID → USE INSTEAD (English)

Avoid Use instead Why
"Therapy for your child" "Practical wellbeing tools for your child" "Therapy" implies licensed clinical treatment
"Treat anxiety" / "cure anxiety" "Support children in managing everyday stress and worry" No treatment claims; keeps scope educational
"Trauma healing" / "trauma therapy" "Building resilience and emotional safety" Trauma treatment requires licensed clinicians
"Hypnosis session" / "hypnotherapy for kids" (Not offered in schools — do not translate, do not soften) See §2.2
"Patients" / "clients" (for families) "Participants" / "families" / "parents" Clinical framing signals medical service
"Diagnose" / "assessment of your child" "Notice signs together" / "understand what your child may be experiencing" Diagnosis is regulated clinical activity
"Fix your child's behavior" "Strengthen communication and reduce daily conflict" Child is never the problem to be fixed
"Mental illness workshop" "Emotional wellbeing workshop" Leads with wellbeing, not illness
"Intervention" "Session" / "workshop" / "program" "Intervention" reads clinical/corrective
"Guaranteed results" "Practical tools families tell us they use at home" Honest, feedback-based claims only (§7)

This mirrors the destigmatizing framing used nationally by MoPH and Embrace.

4. Child Protection & Safeguarding Policy (Draft — Ready to Adapt Per School)

This is a working policy Dorina can hand to any school and adapt to the school's own safeguarding procedures. Lebanese schools operating under MEHE are covered by the MEHE child protection policy (2018) and the national legal framework of Law 422 (2002) on the Protection of Juveniles in Conflict with the Law or at Risk — Dorina's policy explicitly aligns with both and always defers to the school's designated safeguarding lead.

Safeguarding Policy — Dorina [Last Name], Parent & Child Emotional Wellbeing Programs

1. Supervision — never alone with a child.
- Dorina is never alone 1:1 with a child, in any room, at any time, for any reason — including before/after sessions, corridors, and online.
- A member of school staff (teacher, counselor, or designated adult) is present in the room for every child session, from first minute to last.
- Child workshops are group-based only. If a child needs individual attention, the school staff member handles it or the child is referred to the school counselor.

2. Physical and emotional boundaries.
- No physical contact beyond culturally normal, public, staff-witnessed greetings. No lap-sitting, no private hugs, no touch-based exercises.
- No child is ever pressured to share, speak, or participate. Opt-out is always available and never penalized ("pass" is a full answer).
- Activities never ask children to disclose family problems publicly; prompts are skills-based, not confession-based.

3. Photo, video & recording.
- No photography, video, or audio recording of children without separate, explicit, written parental consent (see consent form, §5 — photo consent is its own checkbox, never bundled).
- Children whose parents did not consent are seated outside frame; when in doubt, no faces — hands, materials, and room shots only.
- No child's full name is ever published. School approval is required before any school-identifying image is posted.

4. Data minimization.
- Dorina collects the minimum data needed to run a session: first names for the session only, anonymous feedback forms, and parent contact details given voluntarily by parents themselves (§8).
- No child files, no behavioral records, no health information is collected or stored.

5. Reporting flow — if a child discloses harm or is at risk.
If a child says or shows anything suggesting abuse, neglect, self-harm risk, or danger:

  1. Listen calmly; do not investigate. No leading questions, no promises of secrecy ("I'm really glad you told me. This is important, and I need to share it with someone at school whose job is to help.").
  2. Inform the school's designated safeguarding lead / principal the same day, in person where possible, with a factual written note (what was said/observed, when, verbatim where possible — no interpretation or diagnosis).
  3. The school leads the response. Under the MEHE child protection policy and Law 422, the school's referral pathway (including to judicial/child-protection authorities where legally required) is the official channel. Dorina supports; she does not run a parallel process.
  4. Confidentiality on a need-to-know basis only. The disclosure is never discussed with other parents, staff not involved, or on any group chat.
  5. Referral resources shared with the school/family where appropriate: the school counselor first; licensed child psychologists (§6); and the Embrace National Lifeline — 1564 (Lebanon's national emotional support and suicide prevention helpline) for any emotional crisis.

6. Before every engagement. Dorina requests the school's own child protection policy and the name of its safeguarding lead, and signs the school's code of conduct where one exists.

5.3 Adult participation note — parent workshops

Parent workshops need a lighter touch: a short note on the registration sheet or first slide, not a signed form.

"Welcome. This workshop is educational and coaching-based — it offers practical tools, not therapy or clinical advice. Participation in discussions is always optional, and anything shared in the room stays in the room. If any topic touches something deeper for you, Dorina will gladly point you to licensed professionals and support resources, including the Embrace Lifeline (1564)."

Photos of adult sessions: announce at the start, offer a no-photo zone/side of the room, and never photograph anyone who opts out.

6. Referral Protocol — When a Family Needs Licensed Help

Referring out is not losing a client; it is the behavior that makes counselors, principals, and parents trust Dorina with the next cohort. The rule is absolute: never accept a case beyond scope, even if the family offers to pay privately.

6.1 Signs that suggest licensed support (observational, non-diagnostic)

These are reasons to connect a family with a professional, never labels. Dorina notices and refers; she does not conclude.

  • Talk of self-harm, hopelessness, or "wanting to disappear" (any hint → same-day escalation to school counselor + Embrace Lifeline 1564 shared with the family)
  • Marked, persistent change: withdrawal from friends, loss of interest, prolonged sadness beyond a few weeks
  • Fear or distress that consistently blocks daily life (refusing school, panic-like episodes, persistent sleep problems mentioned by parents)
  • Signs of possible abuse or neglect (→ safeguarding flow, §4.5 — not a referral conversation with the parent first)
  • Aggressive or risk-taking behavior that escalates across sessions
  • Significant regression (soiling, baby talk, clinginess well outside age norms) reported by parents
  • Eating changes, marked weight change, or body-focused distress
  • A parent directly asking for diagnosis, therapy, or medication advice

6.2 How to refer warmly (script)

  1. Route through the school counselor first (in school contexts): "I noticed [specific observation, no labels]. I think [child] deserves more dedicated support than a group workshop can give — could we bring in [counselor name] so the family has the right person alongside them?"
  2. To the parent (with counselor present or per school protocol): "You're doing the right thing by paying attention. What I do is coaching and skills — and I think [child] would really benefit from someone licensed who can go deeper. That's not a bad sign; it's the same as seeing a specialist for anything else that matters."
  3. Hand over a resource, not just advice: name + number of the counselor, one or two licensed child psychologists, and Embrace Lifeline 1564 for any urgent emotional distress.
  4. Close the loop respectfully: check in once with the counselor (not the family's private details) that contact happened. Then step back.

6.3 Referral list (maintain and refresh each term)

Resource Role Notes
School counselor / psychologist (per school) First-line referral inside every partner school Confirm name + contact at contract signature
Embrace National Lifeline — 1564 National emotional support & suicide prevention helpline Free, confidential; include on all parent handouts

Conflict-of-interest rule: Dorina never accepts commissions for referrals and never refers into any service she profits from. Referrals are a duty of care, full stop.

7. Evidence & Claims Policy — Talking About Results Honestly

Dorina's results story is real and strong without a single clinical claim. The honest currency of this practice is participation, satisfaction, and usefulness — not symptom change.

What Dorina CAN say (and should, loudly):
- Participation and engagement numbers: "120 parents across 4 schools attended this term."
- Satisfaction and feedback: "Parents rated the workshop 9.1/10"; "94% said they'd recommend it to another parent."
- Self-reported usefulness: "Most parents told us they used the calm-communication tool at home within the first week."
- Direct quotes (with consent, §7 below) and school testimonials.
- Public data, cited correctly: UNICEF's caregiver-reported figures on children's anxiety and sadness; MoPH's strategy direction on school-linked wellbeing. Always attribute ("According to UNICEF's 2025 assessment…") and never present population statistics as Dorina's own outcomes.

What Dorina NEVER says:
- Before/after mental-health claims: "reduced children's anxiety," "healed," "treated," "children improved clinically."
- Implied diagnosis in stories ("this child had ADHD and after the workshop…").
- Percentage outcome claims on emotional states ("stress down 40%").
- "Scientifically proven" attached to her own workshops (methods can be informed by coaching and psychoeducation practice; her workshops themselves are not clinical trials).

Testimonial rules:
1. Written consent for every named testimonial (a two-line WhatsApp confirmation is acceptable; keep it).
2. Parents may be quoted by first name + "parent, [school type]" — never a child's full name, never a child's face tied to a quote.
3. Children are not quoted as evidence of outcomes; at most, anonymized workshop moments with photo-consented images.
4. School testimonials are approved in writing by the school before publication.
5. No testimonial may contain a clinical claim even if the parent wrote one ("she cured my son's anxiety" → politely edit to "gave us tools that made a real difference at home" with the parent's approval).

8. Confidentiality & Data Handling

Data collected is deliberately minimal; the policy is short because the footprint is small.

Data Source Storage Retention & rules
Anonymous feedback forms (paper/Google Form) Participants One folder/drive, access: Dorina only Keep aggregate scores; shred/delete raw forms after reporting each term
Parent contact list (name, phone/email) Parents opt in themselves Password-protected sheet; never shared Used only for workshop logistics/updates; delete on request immediately
Consent forms Parents via school Physical folder per school + scan Keep for duration of program + 1 year, then destroy [Legal review: retention period]
Session notes Dorina Topic/logistics notes only — no child records No behavioral or personal files on children, ever

Rules of the house:
- Nothing personal flows back to the school beyond the agreed scope. Schools receive aggregate feedback ("32 parents attended, average rating 9/10, top requested topic: sibling conflict") — never "Mrs. X shared that her marriage…" The single exception is the safeguarding flow (§4.5), which overrides confidentiality by design and by law.
- What is shared in the room stays in the room — and Dorina says this out loud at the start of every parent session.
- WhatsApp group etiquette (for any parent follow-up group): school-approved; joining is opt-in; admin posts only or clearly moderated; no discussing individual children; no screenshots shared outside; no marketing spam (max one gentle offer per month); anyone can leave without comment; distress messages answered privately with referral resources (1564), never diagnosed in-chat.
- No data is sold, exchanged, or used for any purpose beyond this practice.

9. Contracts & Liability Basics [All items: review with a Lebanese lawyer/accountant]

Dorina is not expected to become a legal expert — she is expected to never work on a handshake. Minimum standards per engagement:

  1. Written agreement for every engagement, even the free pilot (a 2-page letter of agreement is enough): scope, dates, deliverables, fee, who provides the room/staff/translation, safeguarding clause (staff presence, consent forms), and the non-clinical nature of services stated explicitly.
  2. Scope clause (recommended wording): "Services are educational and coaching-based wellbeing workshops. They do not constitute psychotherapy, medical, or clinical services, and do not replace the school's counseling staff."
  3. Cancellation terms: e.g., ≥7 days' notice — free reschedule; <7 days — 50% fee; <48 hours — full fee; force-majeure carve-out (school closures, security events — reschedule in good faith). Adjust per relationship.
  4. Payment reality: invoice in USD (Lebanon's school market is effectively dollarized); state payment method and timing (e.g., 50% on signature for partnerships, balance per term). [Legal/accounting review: invoicing status, tax registration, NSSF implications, USD/LBP compliance.]
  5. Professional liability insurance: ask a Lebanese broker about professional indemnity / civil liability cover for educational services in schools; some premium schools will ask for proof. [Review with broker + lawyer.]
  6. Intellectual property: workshop materials, slides, and handouts remain Dorina's IP; schools receive a license to use handouts internally; no recording or redistribution of full sessions without written permission.
  7. Use of name/logo: mutual approval clause — the school approves Dorina's use of its name; Dorina approves the school's description of her services (protects against "therapy/hypnotherapy" mislabeling).
  8. Later, if the initiative entity is created (e.g., Nabta — name pending trademark/registry checks), contracts migrate to the entity; until then Dorina contracts personally. [Legal review: entity form, registration, liability separation.]

10. Reputation & Crisis Protocol

Three scenarios, pre-decided. In all cases: respond fast, stay warm, never argue publicly, and loop in the school before saying anything public that involves them.

10.1 A parent complains

Process: acknowledge within 24 hours → listen fully before explaining → involve the school coordinator if the complaint arrived via school → correct anything correctable → follow up in writing → log it.
Holding statement (EN): "Thank you for telling me directly — I take this seriously. I'd like to hear the full picture from you before anything else; can we speak today or tomorrow? Together with the school, I'll make sure this is addressed properly."

10.2 A child becomes distressed in session

Process: the school staff member present takes the child aside gently (never Dorina alone); session continues calmly for the group; counselor and parents informed the same day per school protocol; if any disclosure of harm — safeguarding flow (§4.5); Dorina debriefs with the counselor and notes any activity adjustment.
Holding statement to parents (EN): "During today's session, [child] had a difficult moment, and our staff member supported them right away — this is normal in wellbeing work and was handled with care. The school counselor and I wanted you to hear it from us directly, and we're fully available to talk."

10.3 Media/social media misframes Dorina as a "hypnotist" (or "hypnosis in schools")

Process: do not amplify or debate in comments; one calm correction from Dorina's own channel; notify partner schools proactively the same day so they hear it from her first; ask the outlet for a factual correction; continue posting normal content (the best rebuttal is a visible track record of transparent, staff-present workshops).
Holding statement (EN): "To clarify: my work in schools is coaching-based and educational — group workshops on parenting, confidence, and everyday emotional wellbeing, always with school staff present and full parental consent. Hypnotherapy is part of my professional training background, not something I practice in schools. I'm proud that this work is transparent, and I'm always happy to answer questions."

Escalation rule: anything involving a child's safety, a legal threat, or sustained press attention → pause public comment, call the lawyer, coordinate with the school's leadership before next steps.
