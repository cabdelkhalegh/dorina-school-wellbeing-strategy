Parent & Child Emotional Wellbeing Workshops for Schools in Beirut — Full Offer Architecture

Positioning anchor: Dorina helps schools and families strengthen children's emotional wellbeing through practical parent–child support, coaching-based tools, and bilingual workshops adapted to each school community.

Pricing note (applies to every figure in this document): all prices are working ranges in USD, to validate in first 10 school conversations. Lebanon's school market is largely dollarized post-crisis; ranges will be firmed up after real pricing feedback.

Scope note: Dorina's offer is coaching-based, educational, and non-clinical. It complements — never replaces — school counselors, psychologists, and licensed mental health professionals. Children or families who need clinical support are referred to licensed professionals (e.g., Embrace Lifeline 1564, school counselor pathways).

1. Offer Philosophy — The Pilot-to-Partnership Ladder

The single most important strategic decision in this offer architecture: Dorina is not selling talks. She is selling an ongoing school–family wellbeing capability, delivered through a ladder that starts nearly frictionless and ends in an annual contract.

The four rungs

Rung Offer Price posture Purpose
1. Door-opener 60–90 min parent talk: "Raising Confident Kids in Stressful Times" Free (first 3–5 premium schools) or $150–300 community token fee Get in the room; prove the experience; capture feedback data
2. Paid workshop Single 2h parent workshop or one child series $200–800 (segment-dependent) Convert interest into a first invoice; deepen trust
3. Term package One school term of scheduled sessions Pro-rated from annual packages De-risked commitment for cautious administrations
4. Annual partnership Essentials / Growth / Flagship (Track 3) $2,500–18,000/yr The real business; recurring, calendar-embedded revenue

Why recurring partnerships beat one-off talks

  1. Economics. One-off workshops cap Dorina at ~$400–800 per school interaction and force perpetual re-selling. A single Growth partnership ($6,000–10,000/yr) equals roughly 10–20 individual workshops sold with one negotiation, one contract, and one relationship to maintain. Five partnerships can outperform fifty scattered talks — with far lower sales overhead.
  2. Outcomes. Emotional wellbeing is not built in one evening. Parents change habits through repetition and follow-up; children build confidence over a series, not a session. Schools that buy calendars see behavior change; schools that buy talks see applause. Only the first produces testimonials worth having.
  3. The school's own logic. Beirut's private schools compete on holistic offerings in a dollarized fee market (top-tier fees run ~$14,000–22,000/yr). A named, year-round wellbeing partner is enrollment-marketing material ("our school–family wellbeing program"); a one-time speaker is a forgotten line in a newsletter. The annual calendar makes Dorina part of the school's identity, not its event list.
  4. Defensibility. A partner embedded in the October–June calendar, known to the parent association, and trusted by the counselor is very hard to displace. A speaker is trivially replaceable.
  5. Institutional path. Recurring school contracts are exactly the asset base that justifies the Phase 2 institutional vehicle (naming shortlist pending trademark checks) — an initiative sells calendars; an individual sells evenings.

Operating rule: every rung is designed to sell the next rung. The door-opener talk ends with a feedback form and a booked follow-up meeting. The paid workshop proposal always includes the term/annual option on the same page. The term package renewal conversation is an annual-upgrade conversation.

2. Workshop Catalog — Track 1: Parent Workshops

Track defaults (unless stated): duration 2 hours; capacity up to ~40 parents; bilingual delivery (Arabic-led with English support, or English-led with Arabic support — set per school, see §8); format: interactive seminar with pair exercises, live demonstrations, and a printed take-home tool sheet. Pricing per workshop: premium $400–800; community $200–400; NGO-funded $250–500 (funder-paid) — working ranges in USD, to validate in first 10 school conversations.

P1. Talk So They Listen — Everyday Parent–Child Communication

AR: «كلمات تصل: التواصل اليومي بين الأهل والأبناء»

Most family conflict is not a discipline problem — it is a communication pattern problem. This workshop gives parents a small set of concrete phrasing and listening tools that change the daily tone at home within a week.

  • Audience: parents of children aged 4–15 (mixed welcome)
  • Duration / format / capacity: 2h · interactive seminar · up to 40
  • Learning outcomes — parents will be able to:
    1. Replace the three most common conversation-killers (interrogating, lecturing, dismissing) with connection-first openers
    2. Use reflective listening ("name it to tame it") when a child is upset
    3. Apply the "connect before correct" sequence in real situations
    4. Choose age-appropriate language for hard topics
    5. Run a 10-minute daily "check-in ritual" that fits Lebanese family schedules
  • Sample agenda: 0:00–0:15 welcome, framing, live poll of top frustrations · 0:15–0:35 why children stop talking (patterns, not blame) · 0:35–0:55 tool 1: connection-first openers + pair practice · 0:55–1:15 tool 2: reflective listening demo + practice · 1:15–1:30 tool 3: connect-before-correct, real scenarios from the room · 1:30–1:50 building the daily check-in ritual; troubleshooting objections ("no time," "teens won't talk") · 1:50–2:00 commitments, take-home sheet, feedback form
  • Materials: bilingual tool sheet (1 page), scenario cards, flipchart, projector optional

P2. Calm Starts at Home — Emotional Regulation for the Whole Family

AR: «الهدوء يبدأ من البيت: تنظيم المشاعر في العائلة»

Children borrow their calm from adults — and in stressful times, parents' own regulation is the first tool. This workshop teaches parents to manage their own reactions and coach children through big emotions without shame or escalation.

  • Audience: parents of children aged 3–12 (adaptable to teens)
  • Duration / format / capacity: 2h · interactive seminar with guided practice · up to 40
  • Learning outcomes:
    1. Recognize their own escalation signals before shouting starts
    2. Use two 60-second self-regulation techniques (breath + reframe) in the moment
    3. Co-regulate a distressed child using a 4-step calm sequence
    4. Distinguish "big feelings" from "bad behavior" and respond to each correctly
    5. Set up a family "calm corner" that children actually use
  • Sample agenda: 0:00–0:15 welcome + "what pushes your buttons" anonymous cards · 0:15–0:35 how stress moves through a family (simple, non-clinical model) · 0:35–0:55 parent self-regulation toolkit + guided practice · 0:55–1:15 the 4-step co-regulation sequence, live demo · 1:15–1:35 scenario practice in pairs (tantrum, homework meltdown, sibling fight) · 1:35–1:50 designing the home calm corner; realistic routines · 1:50–2:00 commitments, tool sheet, feedback form
  • Materials: bilingual tool sheet, calm-corner starter checklist, anonymous cards

P3. Firm and Kind — Discipline Without Daily Battles

AR: «حزم وحنان: تربية من دون معارك يومية»

Parents don't have to choose between being obeyed and being close. This workshop replaces the punishment-vs-permissiveness tug-of-war with a clear structure of limits, consequences, and repair that reduces conflict within days.

  • Audience: parents of children aged 3–14; strong fit for community schools
  • Duration / format / capacity: 2h · interactive seminar · up to 40
  • Learning outcomes:
    1. Set limits in one calm sentence instead of repeated warnings
    2. Use natural and logical consequences instead of threats and bribes
    3. De-escalate power struggles with the "two real choices" technique
    4. Repair after conflict without undermining the limit
    5. Align both parents (and grandparents) on one consistent approach
  • Sample agenda: 0:00–0:15 welcome + the discipline myths quiz · 0:15–0:35 why punishment escalates and permissiveness backfires · 0:35–0:55 limit-setting language lab (rewrite real phrases from the room) · 0:55–1:15 consequences that teach: worked examples by age · 1:15–1:35 power-struggle scenarios + two-real-choices practice · 1:35–1:50 the repair conversation; getting the whole household aligned · 1:50–2:00 personal action plan, tool sheet, feedback form
  • Materials: bilingual tool sheet, "phrases that work" pocket card, myths quiz handout

P4. Beside My Anxious Child — Practical Support for Worry and Fear

AR: «إلى جانب طفلي القلق: دعم عملي للمخاوف والقلق»

Anxiety in Lebanese children is documented and widespread — and most parents respond with either reassurance loops or frustration, both of which feed the worry. This workshop gives parents a non-clinical, evidence-informed support role: how to listen, coach brave behavior, and know when to seek professional help.

  • Audience: parents of children aged 5–15; essential in all segments post-2024
  • Duration / format / capacity: 2h · interactive seminar · up to 40 (works well at 20–25 for depth)
  • Learning outcomes:
    1. Recognize how worry shows up by age (stomachaches, avoidance, clinginess, irritability)
    2. Stop the reassurance loop and use validating-plus-coaching language instead
    3. Guide a child through a simple calm-body routine they can use at school
    4. Build a gradual "brave ladder" for avoided situations (school gate, exams, sleep)
    5. Identify the signs that warrant referral to the school counselor or a licensed professional — and how to raise it without stigma
  • Sample agenda: 0:00–0:15 welcome + normalizing (what the data says, without alarm) · 0:15–0:35 how worry works in a child's body and mind (plain-language model) · 0:35–0:55 what helps vs. what feeds it; the reassurance trap · 0:55–1:15 validating-plus-coaching scripts, pair practice · 1:15–1:35 calm-body routine + building a brave ladder, worked example · 1:35–1:50 when and how to seek professional support; referral pathways (counselor, licensed professionals) · 1:50–2:00 commitments, tool sheet, feedback form
  • Materials: bilingual tool sheet, brave-ladder template, referral guidance card (aligned with school counselor)
  • Note: explicitly framed as parental support skills, not treatment of anxiety disorders.

P5. Roots of Confidence — Building Self-Esteem That Lasts

AR: «جذور الثقة: نبني تقدير الذات عند أبنائنا»

Confidence is not built by praise — it is built by competence, contribution, and how adults respond to failure. This workshop shows parents how everyday moments (homework, chores, losing a match) become confidence deposits or withdrawals.

  • Audience: parents of children aged 4–15
  • Duration / format / capacity: 2h · interactive seminar · up to 40
  • Learning outcomes:
    1. Distinguish praise that inflates from feedback that builds ("process praise")
    2. Use failure moments as confidence-building conversations
    3. Assign age-appropriate responsibility that grows real competence
    4. Protect self-esteem from comparison (siblings, grades, social media)
    5. Spot and gently counter negative self-talk in their child
  • Sample agenda: 0:00–0:15 welcome + "confident child" picture exercise · 0:15–0:35 the confidence equation: competence + belonging + safe failure · 0:35–0:55 praise lab: rewriting real praise into process feedback · 0:55–1:15 the failure conversation: scripts and pair practice · 1:15–1:35 responsibility ladder by age; comparison traps (grades, cousins, Instagram) · 1:35–1:50 answering negative self-talk; each parent drafts one weekly confidence ritual · 1:50–2:00 commitments, tool sheet, feedback form
  • Materials: bilingual tool sheet, responsibility ladder handout, praise-rewrite cards

P6. School Stress — A Family Toolkit for Exams, Homework and Pressure

AR: «ضغط المدرسة: أدوات عائلية للامتحانات والواجبات»

The Lebanese curriculum load is heavy, and family evenings often collapse into homework wars and exam dread. This workshop turns parents from pressure amplifiers into pressure regulators — with routines that protect both grades and the relationship.

  • Audience: parents of children aged 8–15; peak demand around exam periods (schedule Nov and May)
  • Duration / format / capacity: 2h · interactive seminar · up to 40
  • Learning outcomes:
    1. Separate the parent's role (structure, calm) from the student's role (the work itself)
    2. Set up a homework routine that ends nightly battles
    3. Use a pre-exam family protocol (sleep, review pacing, morning script)
    4. Respond to a bad grade in a way that improves the next one
    5. Recognize overload and burnout signals and adjust before the child breaks down
  • Sample agenda: 0:00–0:15 welcome + "our evenings look like…" poll · 0:15–0:35 how pressure helps and how it breaks (the inverted-U, in plain language) · 0:35–0:55 the homework contract: roles, place, timing, phones · 0:55–1:15 exam-week family protocol, worked example on a real calendar · 1:15–1:35 the bad-grade conversation: scripts and practice · 1:35–1:50 overload check: signals and adjustments; aligning with teachers · 1:50–2:00 commitments, tool sheet, feedback form
  • Materials: bilingual tool sheet, homework contract template, exam-week planner

P7. The Language of Feelings — Helping Children Express Emotions

AR: «لغة المشاعر: نساعد أطفالنا على التعبير»

"He keeps it all inside" and "she explodes over nothing" are two faces of the same gap: emotional vocabulary. This workshop equips parents to grow their child's feelings language day by day — the foundation skill under confidence, anxiety support, and communication.

  • Audience: parents of children aged 3–12 (with a teen adaptation segment)
  • Duration / format / capacity: 2h · interactive seminar · up to 40
  • Learning outcomes:
    1. Model emotional vocabulary naturally in Arabic and English at home
    2. Use everyday openers (car rides, meals, bedtime) to invite expression without interrogating
    3. Respond to expressed feelings so the child shares more, not less, next time
    4. Use drawing, play, and stories as expression channels for younger or quieter children
    5. Handle "unacceptable" feelings (anger at a sibling, jealousy) without shutting them down
  • Sample agenda: 0:00–0:15 welcome + how many feeling words can we name in 60 seconds (AR+EN) · 0:15–0:35 why expression matters; suppression vs. explosion · 0:35–0:55 vocabulary-building habits: mealtime, bedtime, car-ride rituals · 0:55–1:15 the response that opens vs. the response that closes; pair practice · 1:15–1:35 play, drawing and story techniques (live demo) · 1:35–1:50 hard feelings without punishment; teen adaptations · 1:50–2:00 commitments, tool sheet, feedback form
  • Materials: bilingual feelings-wheel handout (AR/EN), conversation-opener cards, tool sheet

3. Workshop Catalog — Track 2: Child Support Workshop Series

Track defaults: each offer is a series of 4–6 sessions (45–60 min each for ages 6–9; 60 min for 10–12; 60–75 min for 13–15), small groups of 8–15 children, activity-based (games, drawing, movement, role-play, guided discussion). Pricing per series: premium $600–1,200; community $300–600 — working ranges in USD, to validate in first 10 school conversations.

SAFEGUARDING NOTE — applies to every child workshop, non-negotiable:
- A member of school staff (counselor, teacher, or designated safeguarding lead) is present in the room for every session. Dorina never works with a child 1:1 unobserved.
- Written parental consent is collected by the school before session 1 (bilingual consent form provided by Dorina).
- All content is non-clinical: skills, games, and coaching-style tools — no therapy, no diagnosis, no processing of trauma. Any concern about a child is reported to the school's counselor/safeguarding lead per the school's own policy, in the spirit of Law 422 (2002) and MEHE child protection policy.
- Sessions run on school premises during school-approved hours.

C1. The Confidence Club — Self-Confidence Series

AR: «نادي الثقة»

A playful, progressive series where children discover strengths, practice speaking up, and learn that mistakes are part of getting good at things. Children leave with a personal "strengths card" and a habit of trying.

  • Age bands: 6–9 (games and story-based) · 10–12 (challenges and team missions) · 13–15 (identity, voice, and public-speaking micro-challenges)
  • Series: 5 sessions · group 8–15 · school classroom or activity room
  • Learning outcomes:
    1. Name at least three personal strengths with real examples
    2. Use a simple "brave voice" routine to speak in front of the group
    3. Reframe a mistake as information ("what I'd try next")
    4. Give and receive positive peer feedback
    5. Complete a personal challenge chosen in session 1
  • Sample session agenda (session 2, ages 6–9, 45 min): 0:00–0:08 arrival ritual + energy game · 0:08–0:20 story: the character who thought they couldn't · 0:20–0:35 strengths treasure hunt (pair activity) · 0:35–0:43 brave voice circle (each child shares one strength) · 0:43–0:45 sticker/strengths-card update + goodbye ritual
  • Materials: strengths cards, story cards, challenge tracker poster, stickers; staff-present sign-off sheet
  • Capacity: 8–15 per group; multiple groups schedulable per day

C2. My Feelings in Colors — Emotional Expression Series

AR: «مشاعري بالألوان»

Through art, movement, and story, children build a feelings vocabulary in Arabic and English and safe ways to show what's inside. Especially valuable for quieter children and post-stress school communities.

  • Age bands: 6–9 (color, drawing, puppets) · 10–12 (comics, journaling, drama) · 13–15 (creative writing, music, guided discussion)
  • Series: 4 sessions · group 8–15
  • Learning outcomes:
    1. Name and differentiate at least eight feelings (AR + EN)
    2. Match body sensations to feelings ("where do I feel it?")
    3. Express a feeling through a chosen channel (drawing, words, movement)
    4. Use one healthy release strategy for big feelings
    5. Ask a trusted adult for help using a simple script
  • Sample session agenda (session 1, ages 10–12, 60 min): 0:00–0:10 welcome + group agreements (kindness, pass allowed, staff introduced) · 0:10–0:25 feelings wheel game (AR/EN) · 0:25–0:45 comic strip: "a day with a big feeling" · 0:45–0:55 gallery walk + voluntary sharing · 0:55–1:00 calm-breath close + take-home mini card
  • Materials: art supplies, bilingual feelings wheel, comic templates, group-agreement poster
  • Capacity: 8–15 per group

C3. Calm and Brave — Stress & Worry Skills Series (non-clinical)

AR: «هادئ وشجاع»

Children learn what worry feels like, why everybody has it, and a toolkit of calm-body and brave-thinking skills they can use at the school gate, before exams, and at bedtime. Skills-based and normalizing — never diagnostic.

  • Age bands: 6–9 (worry monster games, breathing buddies) · 10–12 (worry vs. facts detective work) · 13–15 (exam stress, thought tools, sleep habits)
  • Series: 6 sessions · group 8–12 (kept smaller deliberately)
  • Learning outcomes:
    1. Explain worry as a normal alarm that sometimes rings too loudly
    2. Use two calm-body techniques (belly breathing, 5-senses grounding) independently
    3. Sort "worry thoughts" from "helpful facts" in everyday examples
    4. Take one small brave step per week toward something avoided
    5. Know which adults to talk to when worry feels too big
  • Sample session agenda (session 3, ages 6–9, 45 min): 0:00–0:08 arrival + weather-of-feelings check-in · 0:08–0:18 review: our calm-breath practice wins · 0:18–0:33 the worry monster shrinks when we name it (drawing activity) · 0:33–0:42 5-senses grounding game · 0:42–0:45 brave-step of the week + goodbye ritual
  • Materials: breathing buddies (soft toys, ages 6–9), worry/fact sorting cards, brave-step tracker
  • Capacity: 8–12 per group
  • Note: children showing significant distress are flagged to the present staff member and school counselor per school policy — referral, never on-the-spot intervention.

C4. Focus Power — Attention & Motivation Series

AR: «قوة التركيز»

A practical series that turns focus into a trainable game: children learn how attention works, build personal study rituals, and connect effort to goals they actually care about.

  • Age bands: 6–9 (attention games, movement breaks) · 10–12 (study rituals, distraction plans) · 13–15 (phones, goal-setting, exam planning)
  • Series: 4 sessions · group 8–15
  • Learning outcomes:
    1. Describe attention as a muscle that trains and tires
    2. Use a personal focus ritual (clear desk, timer, movement break)
    3. Build a simple distraction plan (including phone parking for 13–15)
    4. Break a big task into steps and start with the smallest one
    5. Link effort to a personally chosen goal
  • Sample session agenda (session 2, ages 10–12, 60 min): 0:00–0:10 focus warm-up game (progressively harder) · 0:10–0:25 my distraction map: what steals my attention · 0:25–0:45 build-your-ritual lab (timers, desk setup, break rules) · 0:45–0:55 team challenge applying the ritual · 0:55–1:00 commitment card + close
  • Materials: timers, distraction-map worksheets, ritual-builder cards, commitment cards
  • Capacity: 8–15 per group

C5. Friendship Skills — Social Confidence Series

AR: «مهارات الصداقة»

Role-play-rich sessions on joining groups, sharing, resolving small conflicts, and handling exclusion and teasing — the daily social terrain that shapes how children feel about school.

  • Age bands: 6–9 (turn-taking, joining play, kind words) · 10–12 (conflict repair, gossip, inclusion) · 13–15 (peer pressure, boundaries, online friendship dynamics)
  • Series: 5 sessions · group 8–15
  • Learning outcomes:
    1. Use a friendly "joining in" script for games and groups
    2. Resolve a small conflict with a 3-step repair (stop, say, solve)
    3. Respond to teasing with two calm comeback options
    4. Recognize exclusion and practice one inclusion behavior
    5. Say no to peer pressure while keeping their dignity (10+)
  • Sample session agenda (session 4, ages 6–9, 45 min): 0:00–0:08 hello circle + friendship compliment game · 0:08–0:20 puppet scene: the argument over the game · 0:20–0:35 3-step repair practice in pairs · 0:35–0:42 role-play carousel with coach feedback · 0:42–0:45 friendship pledge + goodbye ritual
  • Materials: puppets (younger bands), scenario cards, role-play props, pledge poster
  • Capacity: 8–15 per group

C6. I Bounce Back — Resilience & Positive Identity Series

AR: «أنهض من جديد»

The capstone series: children build a story of themselves as capable people who can face setbacks — grounded in their strengths, their people, and their culture. Designed to be especially meaningful in community and NGO settings.

  • Age bands: 6–9 (bounce-back heroes, helper maps) · 10–12 (setback stories, strengths shields) · 13–15 (identity, values, "who I'm becoming")
  • Series: 6 sessions · group 8–15
  • Learning outcomes:
    1. Tell a personal "I got through it" story with a beginning, middle, and end
    2. Map their circle of support (family, school, community) and how to activate it
    3. Use self-encouragement phrases instead of self-criticism after setbacks
    4. Identify three personal values or strengths that define them beyond grades
    5. Create a personal "bounce-back plan" for a hard week
  • Sample session agenda (session 5, ages 13–15, 75 min): 0:00–0:10 check-in + music/mood opener · 0:10–0:30 values card sort: what I stand for · 0:30–0:50 my strengths shield (create + explain in pairs) · 0:50–1:05 setback rehearsal: applying the shield to a real scenario · 1:05–1:15 bounce-back plan draft + closing circle
  • Materials: values cards, shield templates, helper-map worksheets, art supplies
  • Capacity: 8–15 per group

5. The Pilot / Door-Opener Offer

Offer name: Raising Confident Kids in Stressful Times — a 60–90 minute evening talk for parents.
AR: «نربّي أولاداً واثقين في زمن الضغوط» — لقاء للأهل (٦٠–٩٠ دقيقة)

Price posture: free for the first 3–5 premium schools (positioned as "we are selecting a small number of Beirut schools for our launch series"); $150–300 token fee for community schools (a token fee protects perceived value and attendance) — working ranges in USD, to validate in first 10 school conversations.

Exact structure (90-min version)

Time Segment
0:00–0:10 School introduces Dorina (intro script provided by us); warm bilingual opening; one-line framing: practical tools, not theory
0:10–0:25 The moment we're in: what Lebanese children are carrying (UNICEF data, delivered with hope, not fear) and why parents are the most powerful protective factor

What the school receives

  • Fully prepared bilingual session (AR/EN mix set with the school in advance)
  • Bilingual take-home tool sheet for every parent (school logo can be added)
  • Promotion kit: WhatsApp invitation text (AR+EN), poster file, reminder message
  • Post-event summary for leadership: attendance, feedback scores, top parent concerns (anonymized)

Conversion mechanism (the whole point)

  1. Feedback form in the room (§8 sample form) — captures satisfaction score, topic votes for future workshops, and an opt-in line: "I would attend a monthly session like this: yes/no."
  2. Topic votes become the proposal. The follow-up proposal to the school opens with their own parents' data: "68% of your parents rated the session 9–10; the top requested topics were school stress and discipline without conflict."
  3. Follow-up meeting booked on the spot. Before leaving the building, Dorina books a 30-minute debrief with the principal/counselor within 7 days ("I'll bring you the parent feedback summary"). The debrief agenda: feedback review → term package or annual partnership proposal → pilot-pricing credit (pilot fee, if paid, is credited against a signed annual package).
  4. 48-hour follow-up email with the summary PDF and the proposal, referencing the booked meeting.

Rule: never leave a pilot without (a) completed feedback forms and (b) a calendared follow-up meeting. A pilot without these is a donated evening, not a door-opener.

7. Pricing Logic & Rate Card

Logic in one paragraph: price anchors to the partnership, not the hour. Single workshops are priced high enough that the annual package is obviously better value per session (a Growth package works out cheaper per delivered event than buying events one by one), pushing schools up the ladder. Premium schools pay top-of-range because the offer feeds their enrollment story in a dollarized fee market; community schools pay accessible mid-range; NGO work is funder-financed at sustainable rates. Dorina's time is protected: nothing is free after the pilot.

Discount policy

  • Never free beyond the pilot. The door-opener is the only free unit, and only for the first 3–5 premium schools. Everything after has a price.
  • Scholarship seats instead of discounts. When a school pleads budget, hold the rate and offer funded inclusion: e.g., "the package stays at $X, and includes 10 scholarship seats in child series for families the counselor nominates." This protects rate integrity, gives the school a story, and serves the mission.
  • Acceptable levers: annual-upfront courtesy adjustment (~5%), pilot-fee credit against a signed annual package, multi-school (same network) pricing on request.
  • Unacceptable: "free this term and we'll see," unpaid "exposure" events after the pilot phase, undocumented verbal discounts.

Explicitly excluded from all school offers

  • 1:1 clinical or therapeutic work with children — not offered in schools under any package. Concerns are referred to the school counselor and licensed professionals. (Private family coaching for parents exists as a separate non-school service, §9 — coaching, not therapy.)
  • Diagnosis, treatment, or medical/psychological assessment of any kind
  • Crisis intervention or replacing the school's counselor/psychologist
  • Sessions with children without staff present and parental consent

8. Delivery Logistics

Bilingual delivery models

Arabic-led, English support Session runs in warm MSA/Lebanese register; slides and tool sheets bilingual; English summaries for key tools Community schools, NGO programs, most parent groups
English-led, Arabic support Session in English; Arabic slide line + Arabic tool sheet; Arabic Q&A welcomed International schools, mixed expat/local parent bodies
Fully mixed Alternating by segment, decided with school; every printed word bilingual Bilingual premium schools

Language model is confirmed with the school at booking, in writing, and printed materials are always bilingual regardless of spoken model.

Group sizes

  • Parent workshops: up to 40 (sweet spot 20–35); door-opener talk can scale to 60–80 with reduced interactivity
  • Child series: 8–15 (8–12 for Calm and Brave); split into multiple groups rather than exceed
  • Teacher mini-sessions: up to 25

Room & tech needs (sent to school as a one-page checklist)

  • Room with movable chairs (circle/pairs possible); child sessions need floor space
  • Projector + screen (parent sessions); flipchart + markers always
  • Microphone for 40+; water; printing of tool sheets by school or delivered by Dorina (cost included)
  • For child sessions: the designated staff member confirmed by name in advance; consent forms returned before session 1

Online fallback (instability contingency)

Given Lebanon's recurring disruptions (security events, strikes, fuel/electricity, weather), every contracted session carries an automatic online fallback, pre-agreed in the contract:

  • Parent workshops → Zoom/Teams webinar version (90 min, adapted interactivity, same tool sheets as PDF); WhatsApp voice-note micro-summary for parents who cannot join live
  • Child series → sessions pause and reschedule by default (online child groups only if the school requests, with staff co-hosting online and consent covering online delivery)
  • Trigger: either party may invoke fallback up to 4 hours before start; no session is "lost" — delivered online or rescheduled within 3 weeks
  • Connectivity reality: sessions designed to survive camera-off/low-bandwidth participation; materials sent in advance

Evaluation & feedback

Every parent session ends with a 2-minute paper (or QR) form; results are aggregated into the school-facing report and Dorina's testimonial pipeline (target NPS ≥ 8.5/10).
