Dorina [Last Name] — Parent & Child Emotional Wellbeing

Brand Strategy: Personal Brand Now, Institutional Initiative Later

Scope note. This document defines the brand system for Dorina's school-facing wellbeing practice in Beirut, Lebanon. It covers vision, two-layer brand architecture, naming, taglines, personality, tone of voice, messaging hierarchy, visual direction (summary — full system in LOGO_BRAND_KIT.md), brand rules, and a rollout roadmap synchronized with 90_DAY_ACTION_PLAN.md (23 July – 25 October 2026). Arabic copy blocks referenced here live in full in BILINGUAL_COPY_BANK.md.

1. Brand Vision & Mission

Vision

Every school community in Lebanon — starting in Beirut — treats children's emotional wellbeing as a shared, everyday practice between home and school, not a crisis response.

Lebanon's children are carrying an extraordinary emotional load. UNICEF's 2025 child-focused rapid assessment found that 72% of caregivers reported their children showing anxiety or nervousness, and 62% reported sadness or depression following the 2024 escalation (UNICEF). At the same time, the Ministry of Public Health's National Mental Health Strategy 2024–2030 explicitly aims to link schools with community wellbeing support (MoPH). The need is documented; the institutional momentum exists; what is missing is a bilingual, parent-focused, coaching-based, non-clinical partner schools can actually hire. That is the space this brand exists to own.

Mission

Dorina helps schools and families strengthen children's emotional wellbeing through practical parent–child support, coaching-based tools, and bilingual workshops adapted to each school community.

This is the fixed core positioning statement. Every asset — LinkedIn header, proposal cover, workshop slide, media bio — should carry this sentence or a close paraphrase of it.

What the brand stands for (strategic pillars)

Pillar What it means in practice
Practical, not theoretical Every workshop ends with tools a parent can use at the dinner table that same evening.
Bilingual by design Arabic and English are equal citizens — sessions, materials, and the brand itself work natively in both.
Complementary, never replacing Dorina works alongside school counselors, psychologists, and NGOs — never as a substitute for licensed clinical care. Referral to licensed professionals is built into the model.
School–family partnership The child is supported best when home and school pull in the same direction. The brand always addresses both.
Warm professionalism Credible enough for a premium school board; warm enough for an anxious parent on a Tuesday evening.

2. Two-Layer Brand Architecture

The brief requires keeping both paths open: Dorina personally as the expert/facilitator, and a future organization/initiative for a more institutional school-facing offer. We formalize this as a deliberate two-layer architecture rather than an undecided fork.

Layer 1 — The Dorina Personal Brand (LIVE NOW)

Name format: Dorina [Last Name] — Parent & Child Emotional Wellbeing (last name placeholder until confirmed).

This is the brand doing the work for the next 6–12 months:

  • Trust is personal in this market. Beirut school directors, counselors, and parent committees buy people they trust before they buy programs. A face, a voice, a track record — that is what opens doors.
  • Speed. A personal brand needs no registration, no logo committee, no legal setup. Dorina can rewrite her LinkedIn profile this week and email a school director on Monday.
  • Credential carrier. Certified practitioner trainings in coaching and therapeutic modalities (Master in Clinical Hypnotherapy, Master in NLP / Coaching, Master in Time Line Therapy) belong to a person, not an entity. They are Dorina's proof, presented as supporting credibility — never the headline.
  • Content engine. LinkedIn, X, PR, and podcast appearances all run on the personal brand. Media wants an expert, not an anonymous initiative.

Layer 1 owns: LinkedIn/X presence, media appearances, the pilot workshops, discovery meetings, thought-leadership content, parent-facing warmth.

Layer 2 — The Initiative (LAUNCH LATER)

Recommended name (pending checks): Nabta | نبتة — School–Family Wellbeing (see §3).

An institutional vehicle serving school contracts:

  • Institutions buy from institutions. An annual partnership contract (the flagship Track 3 offer, $12,000–18,000/yr at the top tier — range to validate) is easier for a school board to approve when the counterparty is a named program with a framework, materials, and an identity — not "a freelancer."
  • Scalability. An initiative can eventually carry associate facilitators, licensed content, teacher-training modules, and NGO project contracts without everything routing through Dorina's calendar.
  • Procurement and funding. NGOs and donor-funded school programs (e.g., in the mold of the UNESCO–MEHE MHPSS program) contract with entities. An initiative name with a mission statement fits a funding proposal; a personal name is harder.
  • Longevity and exit optionality. A brand that outlives any single engagement is an asset Dorina owns.

Layer 2 owns: annual partnership contracts, proposals and MOUs, program frameworks ("the Nabta Wellbeing Calendar"), certificates, NGO/funder-facing documents.

How the two layers connect

The layers are not parallel competing brands; they are one story told at two altitudes:

  • Shared positioning. Both use the same core positioning sentence, the same palette, the same typography, the same tone. Nabta looks like the institutional expression of Dorina's practice — because it is.
  • Endorsement flows upward at first, downward later. In year one, Dorina's personal credibility endorses the initiative ("founded by Dorina [Last Name]"). Over time, the initiative's track record ("delivered in 12 Beirut schools") endorses Dorina back.
  • Combined signature. Once Layer 2 launches, all formal communications use:

Dorina [Last Name], Founder of Nabta — School–Family Wellbeing

This signature is the hinge of the whole architecture: personal trust + institutional weight in one line. It appears in email signatures, proposal covers, LinkedIn headline (secondary line), speaker bios, and certificates.

When to launch Layer 2 — the trigger

Do not launch the initiative on day one. Launch it when there is proof to pour into it. The defined trigger:

Launch Layer 2 when 2–3 school partnerships are signed (any tier — Essentials, Growth, or Flagship).

Rationale: at that point (a) there are testimonials and delivery photos to populate an institutional identity, (b) recurring contracts justify the legal/registration cost, (c) the name can debut as "the initiative behind the programs at [School A] and [School B]" instead of an empty shell. Based on the 90-day plan's KPI of one signed partnership by day 90, the realistic Layer 2 launch window is Q1 2027, with naming, trademark, and registry checks completed beforehand (see roadmap, §9).

Until the trigger: use the descriptive umbrella "Parent & Child Emotional Wellbeing Workshops for Schools in Beirut" (or the premium variant "School–Family Emotional Wellbeing Programs") on proposals — descriptive, credible, and requiring no registration.

3. Naming

Universal caveat (repeat on every naming discussion): No name below has been verified for trademark availability, Lebanese commercial-registry availability, or domain/social-handle availability. All options require checks with a Lebanese IP/commercial lawyer and registry searches before any public use, printing, or registration. Treat every recommendation as "recommended pending checks."

3.1 Personal brand naming (Layer 1)

Option Form Assessment
Dorina [Last Name] (full name) "Dorina [Last Name] — Parent & Child Emotional Wellbeing" Recommended. Full names read as professional and search-distinct; essential for LinkedIn, PR, and proposals.
Dorina (first name only) "Dorina — Emotional Wellbeing for Families" Warm and memorable for parent-facing/social contexts; too informal alone for school boards and media. Use as the spoken short form, not the formal identity.
Coach Dorina / Ms. Dorina Title-prefixed Avoid as brand name. "Coach" narrows perception; titles date quickly. Facilitators may naturally be called "Ms. Dorina" in workshops — fine verbally, not on paper.

Descriptor line. Always pair the name with the descriptor Parent & Child Emotional Wellbeing — the name says who, the descriptor says what, and neither leads with any therapy modality.

Arabic transliteration. Standardize دورينا for "Dorina" (natural, unambiguous, matches the fixed Arabic core line). The last name's Arabic spelling must be confirmed with Dorina herself — family-name transliterations in Lebanon often have an established French/English spelling and a preferred Arabic form; pick one of each and never vary it (consistency matters for search and for school records). Rules once fixed:

  • One Latin spelling, one Arabic spelling, everywhere (LinkedIn, WhatsApp, certificates, invoices).
  • Arabic-first documents: دورينا [اسم العائلة] with the Latin form in parentheses on first mention.
  • Never mix scripts within one lockup line.

3.3 Recommendation

Nabta | نبتة — full form: Nabta — School–Family Wellbeing | نبتة — برنامج التوازن العاطفي بين المدرسة والعائلة

Why Nabta wins:

  1. Metaphor with a job. A seedling needs soil (family), light (school), and consistent care (the program). The metaphor explains the three-track model without a single extra word, and it feeds directly into the recommended "Seedling Embrace" logo concept (see LOGO_BRAND_KIT.md).
  2. Bilingual grace. "Nabta" is effortless in English and beautiful in Arabic — rare in this shortlist.
  3. Clean risk profile. Unlike Sanad and Jouzour, no known Lebanese institutional collision — though this is precisely what the formal checks must confirm.
  4. Room to grow. "Nabta Wellbeing Calendar," "Nabta Parent Circles," "Nabta Teacher Sessions" — the name extends naturally into product naming.

Runner-up: Ghars (if Nabta fails checks). Eliminate early: Sanad and Jouzour unless checks surprise us. Keep: the English descriptive fallback as the permanent explanatory sub-line under Nabta.

Pre-launch checklist for the chosen name: Lebanese trademark search (Ministry of Economy & Trade, relevant Nice classes — 41 education/training at minimum) → commercial registry search → domain check (nabta-wellbeing.com or similar; exact .com likely taken for a common noun — a modified domain is acceptable) → Instagram/LinkedIn/Facebook handle check → Google search in AR and EN for confusable organizations in education/health.

5. Brand Personality & Archetype

Archetype: Caregiver–Sage blend (approx. 60/40)

  • Caregiver (dominant): protects, nurtures, reassures. This is why parents come and why schools trust her around children. Expressed as: warmth, patience, "you are not a bad parent" energy, safety-first practice.
  • Sage (supporting): knows, teaches, clarifies. This is why premium schools pay. Expressed as: frameworks, evidence-aware language, calm authority, structured programs.

The blend produces the brand's signature feel: "confident care." Never clinical coldness (pure Sage risk), never soft vagueness (pure Caregiver risk).

Personality in five adjectives: warm, practical, credible, bilingual, steady.

Never: alarmist, preachy, mystical, clinical, salesy.

Six tone-of-voice rules (with do/don't pairs)

Rule 1 — Lead with the child and the family, never with the modality. (school-facing)
- ✅ Do: "This workshop gives parents practical tools to support anxious children at home and reinforce what your counselors are doing at school."
- ❌ Don't: "As a Master in Clinical Hypnotherapy, Dorina uses hypnotherapy and NLP techniques with your students."

Rule 2 — Speak plainly; translate every concept into a kitchen-table sentence. (parent-facing)
- ✅ Do: "You'll learn what to say — and what not to say — when your child comes home upset about school."
- ❌ Don't: "Participants will acquire co-regulation competencies and psychoeducational frameworks for affect management."

Rule 3 — Reassure, never alarm. No fear-based marketing, ever. (social)
- ✅ Do: "Most children argue about homework. Here are three ways to lower the temperature tonight."
- ❌ Don't: "Is your child silently suffering? Ignoring these warning signs could damage them for life."

Rule 4 — Stay firmly non-clinical; support and refer, never diagnose or treat. (all contexts)
- ✅ Do: "These are wellbeing tools, not therapy. When a child needs specialized care, we help families connect with licensed professionals."
- ❌ Don't: "We treat childhood anxiety and can resolve trauma in a few sessions."

Rule 5 — Honor both languages equally; write Arabic warm and natural, never machine-literal. (parent-facing / social)
- ✅ Do: "كيف نساعد أولادنا على التعبير عن مشاعرهم من دون خوف؟ ورشة عملية للأهل باللغتين العربية والإنجليزية."
- ❌ Don't: English post with a stiff word-for-word Arabic translation pasted underneath ("الورشة سوف تقوم بتمكين الوالدين من أجل إدارة العواطف").

Rule 6 — Show partnership, not performance; the school is the hero of its own story. (school-facing)
- ✅ Do: "Your school already invests in student wellbeing — this program extends that care into families' homes."
- ❌ Don't: "Schools are failing to address the emotional crisis. Dorina fixes what your staff can't."

6. Messaging Hierarchy

Four nested levels, each expanding the last. English masters below; Arabic versions live in BILINGUAL_COPY_BANK.md.

Level 1 — One-liner (≤15 words)

Bilingual parent & child emotional wellbeing workshops and year-round school partnerships in Beirut.

Use: LinkedIn headline, email signature line 2, social bios, one-pager header.

Level 2 — Elevator pitch (~60 words, 20 seconds spoken)

Dorina helps schools and families strengthen children's emotional wellbeing through practical parent–child support, coaching-based tools, and bilingual workshops adapted to each school community. She runs parent workshops, child confidence and resilience sessions, and year-round school wellbeing partnerships across Beirut — in Arabic and English — always working alongside school counselors, never replacing them. It starts with a single pilot workshop.

Use: discovery meetings, phone introductions, podcast intros, "About" openers.

Level 3 — 100-word boilerplate

Dorina [Last Name] is a Beirut-based parent–child emotional wellbeing facilitator working with schools, parents, and children across Lebanon in Arabic and English. Her workshops give parents practical tools for calmer communication, confident discipline, and supporting anxious children — and give children skills for self-esteem, emotional expression, and resilience. Beyond single workshops, Dorina partners with schools on year-round wellbeing programs: monthly parent sessions, child workshops, teacher–parent collaboration, and annual wellbeing calendars. Her training includes certified practitioner qualifications in coaching and therapeutic modalities — Master in Clinical Hypnotherapy, Master in NLP / Coaching, and Master in Time Line Therapy. Her approach is practical, non-clinical, and complementary to school counseling teams.

Use: proposal "About" sections, press releases, event programs, website About page.

Level 4 — Full bio (~220 words)

Dorina [Last Name] is a parent–child emotional wellbeing facilitator based in Beirut, Lebanon, working in Arabic and English with schools, parent communities, and children.

Her work answers a question nearly every Lebanese school is asking today: how do we support children's emotional wellbeing when families are stretched and pressures on children keep rising? Dorina's answer is practical and partnership-based. For parents, she delivers workshops on calmer communication, emotional regulation at home, discipline without constant conflict, and supporting anxious children. For children, she runs small-group sessions building self-confidence, emotional expression, focus, and resilience — always with school staff present and full parental consent. For schools, she designs year-round wellbeing partnerships: monthly parent sessions, child workshop series, teacher–parent collaboration sessions, awareness campaigns, and an annual wellbeing calendar tailored to each community.

Dorina's training includes certified practitioner qualifications in coaching and therapeutic modalities: Master in Clinical Hypnotherapy, Master in NLP / Coaching, and Master in Time Line Therapy. She works in a strictly non-clinical, trauma-sensitive frame — offering tools and support, not diagnosis or treatment — and refers families to licensed professionals whenever specialized care is needed.

Her programs adapt to each school's context, from premium private and international schools to community schools and NGO education programs, and are delivered bilingually to meet each audience where it is.

Use: speaker profiles, media kits, website, long-form proposals.

Hierarchy rule: lower levels must never contradict higher ones; credentials appear from Level 3 onward only, always after the value story, always with the exact wording.

7. Visual Identity Direction (Summary)

Full specifications, logo concepts, and production rules live in LOGO_BRAND_KIT.md. Summary for strategic alignment:

  • Palette: Deep Teal #1F6F6B (trust/calm — primary), Warm Sand #E8DCC8 (warmth — background), Apricot #F2A65A (child energy — accent), Ink #2B2B2B (text), Soft White #FAF7F2 (base). Deliberately not clinical blue-and-white, not primary-color kindergarten branding.
  • Typography: IBM Plex Sans (EN) + IBM Plex Sans Arabic (AR) — a genuinely harmonized bilingual pair; Fraunces as optional display serif for premium collateral warmth.
  • Logo direction: an abstract mark combining a growing leaf/seedling with an embracing curve (parent/child) — recommended concept "Seedling Embrace." No medical symbols (no brain, no cross, no couch), no childish clip-art. Must perform in AR-only, EN-only, and bilingual lockups.
  • Visual tone: warm-professional; real photography of Beirut classrooms and families (with strict consent rules) over stock clichés.
  • One system, two layers: Layer 1 (Dorina) and Layer 2 (Nabta) share palette and type; Nabta adds the symbol as its primary mark, so the future launch feels like a natural growth of the existing identity, not a rebrand.

8. Brand Dos & Don'ts

# ✅ Do ❌ Don't
1 Lead every first line with parenting, emotional wellbeing, coaching, resilience, child confidence, or school–family partnership Never mention hypnotherapy in the first line (or headline, or subject line) of any school-, parent-, or public-facing material
2 State credentials exactly: Master in Clinical Hypnotherapy; Master in NLP / Coaching; Master in Time Line Therapy — framed as "certified practitioner trainings in coaching and therapeutic modalities," appearing after the value story Alter, abbreviate, or misspell credentials ("Master NPL," "time lie therapy"); imply medical licensure
3 Use non-clinical language: "support," "tools," "skills," "wellbeing"; refer families to licensed professionals when needed Claim diagnosis, treatment, therapy for medical conditions, trauma treatment, or any replacement of school counselors/psychologists
4 Follow child-protection practice: school staff present in child workshops, parental consent forms, alignment with school safeguarding policy and the spirit of Law 422 (2002) Work 1:1 with a child unobserved; photograph children without written consent; store child data casually
5 Present prices as ranges "to validate in the first conversations" Publish fixed public price lists before market validation
6 Caveat all initiative names as "pending trademark and commercial-registry checks" Print, register, or publicly launch "Nabta" (or any name) before checks clear
7 Write Arabic as natural, warm MSA appropriate for Lebanon Publish machine-literal Arabic; fake-italicize or stretch Arabic script
8 Use the fixed palette, fonts, and one consistent name spelling per script Use clinical blue-white schemes, alarm red, medical symbols, or vary name spellings between assets
9 Frame NGOs (Himaya, Embrace, War Child, Right To Play) and school counselors as partners and referral allies Position against them as competitors, or criticize schools' current wellbeing efforts
10 Keep every asset Dorina-specific and this-project-specific Reference any other project, client, or venture in any Dorina material
