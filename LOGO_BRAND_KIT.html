Dorina [Last Name] — Parent & Child Emotional Wellbeing

Logo Concepts, Color, Typography, Usage Rules & Production Kit

Scope note. This is the visual identity companion to BRAND_STRATEGY.md. It serves the Layer 1 personal brand now and is pre-built to extend into the Layer 2 initiative (recommended name Nabta | نبتة, pending trademark/commercial-registry checks) without a rebrand. Everything here can be handed directly to a designer or used with AI image tools (§6).

1. Design Brief

Who the identity must convince

Audience Context of first contact What the identity must signal
School directors & boards (premium private/international) Emailed proposal PDF, LinkedIn profile Credible, established, safe to put in front of parents; worth a paid contract
School counselors & coordinators One-pager, meeting handout Professional peer, complementary to their work, non-clinical
Parents (community + premium) WhatsApp avatar/flyer, Instagram, workshop slides Warm, trustworthy, "for people like me," bilingual
NGO / program officers Proposal documents, partnership decks Mission-aligned, ethical, structured
Media / podcast producers LinkedIn banner, press bio headshot A clear expert with a clear topic

Where it must perform (priority contexts)

  1. School proposal covers and one-pagers (A4 print + PDF) — the money context.
  2. WhatsApp avatar — tiny circle, ~128 px; most Beirut parent communication happens here.
  3. LinkedIn banner (1584×396) and profile.
  4. Workshop slides (16:9), projected in variable classroom lighting.
  5. Certificates of participation for parents (print, premium feel).
  6. Instagram tiles, email signature, feedback forms, name badge.

The feel

"Confident care." Warm-professional. Calm, organic, growing. Think: a well-designed family education foundation — not a hospital, not a nursery, not a corporate consultancy.

Hard exclusions: no clinical/medical symbols (no brain, no cross, no couch, no stethoscope, no heartbeat line), no childish clip-art (no crayon fonts, no cartoon children, no rainbows), no fear imagery, no hypnosis imagery (no spirals, no pendulums, no closed eyes) — the brand never leads with hypnotherapy and the logo must not either.

2. Three Logo Concepts

All three share the palette (§3), the type system (§4), and the requirement to work in EN-only, AR-only, and bilingual lockups. Concept A is the recommended direction per the agreed foundation.

Concept A — "Seedling Embrace" ⭐ RECOMMENDED

Metaphor. A young seedling (the child) growing inside an embracing curve (the parent — and, at the institutional layer, the school community). Growth held by care. This is the exact agreed direction: abstract mark combining a growing leaf/seedling + embracing curve, and it pre-loads the Nabta ("seedling") name for Layer 2 — one mark serves both layers.

Composition & geometry.
- Built on a circle grid. The embracing curve is a thick, open arc — roughly 270° of a circle, drawn with a stroke weight ~1/5 of the mark's diameter, opening at the upper right (openness = non-institutional warmth; an unbroken circle would feel closed/clinical).
- The arc's terminal at the bottom tapers and curls slightly inward, like an arm ending in a gentle hold.
- From the lower third of the enclosed space, the seedling rises: a single slim stem curving 8–10° off vertical (organic, not rigid), carrying two leaves — one smaller, lower leaf and one larger, upper leaf. Two leaves = parent and child; also reads as a sprout's first true leaves.
- Leaves are drawn as vesica-piscis (lens) shapes from the same circle grid, so curve tension matches the arc — the whole mark feels like one family of curves.
- Negative space: the gap between the upper leaf and the arc opening forms a subtle upward "release" — the child growing beyond the embrace, not trapped inside it. In the well-balanced version, the space between stem and arc on the left suggests a person's profile leaning in (do not force this; if it emerges from the geometry, keep it).
- Color logic: arc in Deep Teal #1F6F6B; leaves in Apricot #F2A65A (upper) and Deep Teal (lower) — or both teal with an apricot stem-tip for restraint. On dark backgrounds: arc in Soft White #FAF7F2, leaves in Apricot.

Primary lockup. Mark left, wordmark right (horizontal): "Dorina [Last Name]" in IBM Plex Sans SemiBold, Ink #2B2B2B, with the descriptor "Parent & Child Emotional Wellbeing" in Regular, Deep Teal, letterspaced +2%, on a second line. Mark height = 2.2× the name's cap height. A stacked version (mark above centered text) serves square formats.

Bilingual AR/EN lockup behavior.
- Bilingual formal (proposal covers, certificates): mark centered; EN wordmark to the left of the mark reading toward it, AR wordmark دورينا [اسم العائلة] (IBM Plex Sans Arabic SemiBold) to the right — each language reads toward the shared symbol. Descriptors below in respective languages.
- AR-first contexts: mirror the horizontal lockup — mark on the right, Arabic wordmark to its left (RTL reading order starts at the mark). The mark itself is not mirrored: its opening stays upper-right in all versions (asymmetric organic marks may keep one orientation; mirroring would change the leaf gesture).
- Arabic descriptor: التوازن العاطفي للأهل والأبناء (natural MSA, warm register).
- Never stack Arabic above English in the same lockup with different visual weight — equal size, equal weight, always.

Monochrome. Single-color versions in Ink, Deep Teal, or Soft White (reversed). The mark is pure solid shapes — no gradients, no overlaps requiring transparency — so one-color reproduction (fax-grade, embroidery, stamp) is lossless by design.

Small-size behavior. At ≤32 px (WhatsApp avatar, favicon): use the mark only, no text, on a Warm Sand #E8DCC8 or Deep Teal circle. Below 24 px, switch to the simplified cut: arc + single leaf (drop the smaller leaf), stroke weights bumped +15%. Test target: recognizable at 16 px favicon.

Why it wins: direct match to the agreed direction; unique metaphor (embrace + growth in one gesture); scales from favicon to banner; already is the Nabta symbol when Layer 2 launches.

Concept B — "Two Voices"

Metaphor. Parent–child communication as the heart of wellbeing: two abstract speech forms — one larger, one smaller — leaning toward each other, together forming a soft implied heart. Conversation as care.

Composition & geometry.
- Two rounded asymmetric speech-bubble forms built from superellipse curves (no sharp bubble "tails" — instead, each form has one softly drawn-out corner pointing toward the other, like turned heads).
- The larger form (parent, Deep Teal) sits lower-left; the smaller (child, Apricot) upper-right, overlapping the larger by ~15%. The overlap zone is the key: rendered in a blended deeper shade (or, in flat production, a cut showing Warm Sand background) shaped as a leaf/seed — what the conversation grows.
- Negative space: the outer silhouette of the two forms together approximates a tilted heart — felt, not literal. The gap beneath the child form gives it "lift," as if rising.
- Corner radii from a shared radius scale (r, 1.6r, 2.5r) so both forms feel related, like family.

Primary lockup. Stacked: mark above, "Dorina [Last Name]" centered beneath in IBM Plex Sans SemiBold, descriptor line under it. The two-form mark is wider than tall, so horizontal lockups place it left at 2× cap height.

Bilingual behavior. Same rules as Concept A (mark-anchored, RTL mirror of layout only). A pleasant accident: in AR-first layouts with the mark on the right, the smaller "child" form naturally leads the reading eye. The two-voices metaphor is itself bilingual — the two forms can subtly stand for the two languages in bilingual campaign use.

Monochrome. The overlap must switch from blended color to a white (background) gap of 1.5% of mark width between the forms, with the leaf shape knocked out. Slightly more production care than Concept A.

Small-size behavior. Good at 32 px; at ≤24 px the overlap leaf disappears — use the two forms with a knockout gap only. Weakest of the three at favicon size because two shapes + gap demand more pixels.

Risks. Speech-bubble marks are common in coaching/communication branding (differentiation depends on execution quality); metaphor centers Track 1 (parent communication) more than the school-partnership story; does not connect to the Nabta name — Layer 2 would need a symbol evolution.

Concept C — "Rising Sun School"

Metaphor. A sunrise over an open book that doubles as a horizon line: new beginnings, learning, hope — wellbeing dawning over the school day. Speaks most directly to institutions.

Composition & geometry.
- Lower element: an open book drawn as two gentle upward arcs meeting at a shallow center V — deliberately minimal so it reads equally as book and horizon (and, at a squint, as open welcoming hands — a valued third reading).
- Upper element: a half-sun — solid semicircle in Apricot #F2A65A — rising from behind the center of the book/horizon, offset slightly left of center for organic asymmetry.
- Rays: three short rounded-cap strokes fanned above the sun at 30°/90°/150°. Three rays = the three tracks (parents, children, school partnership) — a quiet internal story for the pitch deck.
- Negative space: the gap between sun and book arcs is a constant channel (breathing room = calm); the V between the book arcs points up at the sun — subtle "lifting" gesture.
- Book/horizon in Deep Teal; sun and rays Apricot; on dark backgrounds, book in Soft White.

Primary lockup. Naturally wide and grounded — strongest as a stacked mark above centered text (title-page energy). Horizontal lockup works but the mark needs 2.4× cap height to keep ray detail.

Bilingual behavior. Fully symmetric mark — identical in LTR and RTL layouts with zero adjustments (its unique advantage). Bilingual certificate lockup: mark centered top, EN line left-aligned left column, AR line right-aligned right column, mirrored margins.

Monochrome. Solid shapes, clean one-color. Caution: in monochrome the sun semicircle can read as a setting sun in bottom-heavy compositions — always keep the mark upper-positioned on the page.

Small-size behavior. At ≤32 px drop the rays (they clog); sun + book alone still read. At 16 px it risks reading as a generic "sunrise icon" — the most generic of the three at tiny sizes.

Risks. Sunrise-over-book is a well-worn education-sector trope (many school and ministry logos nearby — an originality search is a must if chosen); it says "school/education" louder than "family/care," under-serving the parent-facing half of the brand; no link to the Nabta name.

Recommendation

Proceed with Concept A — "Seedling Embrace" as the primary identity; keep Concept B as the alternate if stakeholders want a communication-centered story. Concept C is the fallback for a strictly institutional audience but is the least differentiated. Decision gate: Dorina + AK review AI-generated drafts (§6) of all three before commissioning final vectors.

3. Color System

Palette (exact — do not resample or "improve")

Role Name HEX Emotional job
Primary Deep Teal #1F6F6B Trust, calm, credibility — the brand's anchor
Background Warm Sand #E8DCC8 Warmth, humanity, softness
Accent Apricot #F2A65A Child energy, optimism, highlights
Text Ink #2B2B2B Body text, quiet authority (never pure black #000000)
Base Soft White #FAF7F2 Page/screen base — warm, not clinical white

60 / 30 / 10 usage rule

  • 60% — Soft White + Warm Sand. Page grounds, slide backgrounds, breathing space. Soft White for text-heavy surfaces; Warm Sand for section bands, sidebars, pull-quote panels.
  • 30% — Deep Teal. Headlines, the logo, cover bands, table headers, footer bars, primary buttons. Teal is the recognition color: a school should associate "that calm teal" with Dorina.
  • 10% — Apricot. Strictly rationed: one accent per view — a key statistic, an icon, the active step, a divider, the CTA. If a layout has three apricot elements, remove two. Scarcity is what makes it feel like child energy rather than noise.
  • Ink rides along as text and doesn't count in the ratio.

Accessibility & contrast (WCAG 2.1 targets)

Combination Approx. ratio Verdict
Ink #2B2B2B on Soft White #FAF7F2 ~13:1 ✅ Body text — excellent
Ink on Warm Sand #E8DCC8 ~9:1 ✅ Body text on sand panels
Deep Teal #1F6F6B on Soft White ~5.4:1 ✅ Text at any size (AA)
Soft White on Deep Teal ~5.4:1 ✅ Reversed headlines, buttons
Apricot #F2A65A on Soft White ~2.1:1 ❌ Never for text. Graphics/large decorative shapes only
Apricot on Deep Teal ~2.6:1 ⚠️ Large display text (≥24 px bold) and graphic elements only — verify per use
Ink on Apricot ~6:1 ✅ Small labels/badges on apricot chips

Rules: body text is Ink on Soft White/Warm Sand, or Soft White on Deep Teal — nothing else. Apricot is a shape color, not a text color (exception: Ink text on apricot chips). Run final artwork through a contrast checker (e.g., WebAIM) before print/publish — the ratios above are working values for design decisions.

Colors to avoid — and why

  • Clinical blue-white (hospital blues #0057B8-family + stark #FFFFFF): reads as medical/pharma — the exact association this non-clinical brand must never trigger.
  • Alarm red / warning orange-red (#D32F2F-family): fear-based signaling; contradicts the no-alarm tone rule. Even feedback forms use teal, not red, for "needs attention."
  • Pure black #000000: harsh; always Ink #2B2B2B.
  • Neon/candy brights and rainbow sets: childish clip-art territory.
  • Grays as a scheme: cold/corporate; warmth must survive even in "neutral" layouts — use Warm Sand tints instead of gray.

4. Typography

Type stack

Use Latin (EN) Arabic (AR) Weights licensed/used
Everything (default) IBM Plex Sans IBM Plex Sans Arabic Light 300, Regular 400, Medium 500, SemiBold 600, Bold 700
Display option (premium collateral: certificates, proposal covers, event titles) Fraunces (soft serif) — (no forced Arabic serif; use IBM Plex Sans Arabic SemiBold instead) SemiBold 600, Bold 700
Fallbacks (email/docs where fonts can't embed) Segoe UI / Arial Segoe UI (Arabic) / Tahoma —

IBM Plex Sans + IBM Plex Sans Arabic are a genuinely harmonized pair (matched weights, compatible proportions) and are free under the Open Font License — bilingual consistency with zero licensing cost. Fraunces adds warmth to premium EN moments; never use it for body text or UI.

Scale (print A4 / slides)

Level Size (A4 print) Size (16:9 slides) Weight
H1 / cover title 28–34 pt 40–48 pt SemiBold (or Fraunces SemiBold, EN premium)
H2 / section 18–22 pt 30–34 pt SemiBold
H3 / sub 13–15 pt 22–26 pt Medium
Body 10.5–11.5 pt 18–22 pt Regular
Caption / footnote 8.5–9 pt 14–16 pt Regular, Deep Teal or 70% Ink

Line-height: 1.45–1.6 for Latin body; headlines 1.15–1.25.

Arabic typesetting rules (non-negotiable)

  1. Never fake-italicize Arabic. No oblique/skew transforms, ever — slanting breaks the script's structure and reads as broken. For emphasis in Arabic use weight (Medium/SemiBold) or Deep Teal color instead.
  2. Never letterspace (track) Arabic. Tracking breaks the connected letterforms. Latin small-caps letterspacing tricks have no Arabic equivalent — don't attempt one.
  3. Line-height: Arabic needs more vertical room — set AR body at 1.6–1.8 (vs 1.45–1.6 Latin) so ascenders, dots, and diacritics never clip.
  4. Size compensation: at equal point size Arabic appears slightly smaller; set AR +5–10% when it sits beside Latin in one layout, and optically align baselines.
  5. Mirrored layouts: AR documents are full RTL mirrors — text right-aligned, margins mirrored, logo lockup at top-right, page numbers mirrored, tables reading right-to-left, arrows/progress icons flipped. (The logo mark itself is never mirrored — §2.)
  6. Numerals: use Western Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3) consistently in both languages — standard for Lebanon and keeps prices/dates identical across versions.
  7. Punctuation: Arabic comma «،» and question mark «؟» in AR text; never Latin punctuation inside Arabic sentences.
  8. Bilingual pages: prefer two mirrored columns (EN left-aligned left, AR right-aligned right) or clean stacked blocks — never interleave languages line-by-line in one paragraph.
  9. Warm MSA register: typography carries voice — generous line-height, restrained weight contrast, no ALL-CAPS-equivalent shouting in either script.

5. Usage Rules

Clear space

Minimum clear space around any lockup = height of the leaf element ("x") on all four sides (fallback: 50% of mark height). Nothing enters this zone — no text, photos, page edges, other logos. On partner/school co-branded materials, separate logos by at least 2x with a 1 px Warm Sand-tint divider if needed.

Minimum sizes

Asset Minimum
Full horizontal lockup (print) 30 mm wide
Full lockup (screen) 140 px wide
Stacked lockup 22 mm / 110 px
Mark only 8 mm / 24 px
Mark, simplified cut (single leaf) 16 px (favicon floor)

Below minimums: drop to the next simpler asset (lockup → stacked → mark → simplified mark). Never scale below floor.

Forbidden alterations

Never: stretch/squash or rotate; recolor outside the palette; add gradients, bevels, drop shadows, outlines, or glows; place on busy photos without the approved scrim (Deep Teal at 80% or Warm Sand at 90%); redraw or "adjust" curves; change fonts in the lockup; mirror the mark; attach taglines into the lockup (taglines live in layout, not inside the logo); crop the mark; use old drafts after final approval; pair with any medical symbol or other project's branding.

Photography style

  • Real over stock. Warm, natural-light photos of real Beirut classrooms, workshops, and families. Golden-hour warmth, Warm Sand/teal-compatible tones; avoid cold fluorescent casts (correct white balance toward warm).
  • Candid over posed. Hands raised in a workshop, a parent listening, children collaborating — not staged smiles at the camera.
  • Diverse and local. Reflect the real mix of Beirut school communities — veiled and unveiled mothers, fathers present (fathers are chronically missing from parenting imagery — include them deliberately), varied ages and backgrounds.
  • Banned clichés: head-in-hands "stressed parent" stock, sad child in dark corner, brain graphics, therapy-couch scenes, generic multi-ethnic-circle-of-hands stock, anything watermarked.
  • Child-photo consent rule (absolute): no identifiable child appears in any material without a signed parental/guardian consent form specifying the exact use, and alignment with the school's own safeguarding/media policy. Default composition style: children from behind, hands-and-activity crops, or defocused backgrounds — so most materials need no identifiable children at all. When in doubt, shoot the parents and the room, not the kids.
  • Duotone treatment (Deep Teal shadows / Warm Sand highlights) is approved for banners and social tiles where photo quality varies.
